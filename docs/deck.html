Saksi

A confidential holder register for tokenized real-world assets. Every position witnessed, no position revealed.

01
The problem

Tokenization made the holder register public.
Institutions never agreed to that.

An institution that buys a tokenized note broadcasts its position size, its entry timing, and its counterparty to every competitor watching the chain.

What leaks today

  • Position size, per holder, per asset
  • Entry and exit timing, to the block
  • Counterparty graph across the whole book
  • Concentration — who the anchor investor is

What it costs

A public register is a standing disclosure obligation no regulator asked for and no allocator wants. So institutional RWA retreats to permissioned ledgers, where the liquidity is not — or the issuance never happens at all.

Of the projects building on Cleanverse infrastructure this cycle, every one publishes its holder positions in the clear.

02
Why this is still unsolved

The obvious fix breaks the thing that made it legal.

Public register

Compliant, not confidential.

The issuer can prove eligibility, enforce transfer restrictions, and answer a regulator — but every holder trades in the open.

Privacy pool

Confidential, not compliant.

Positions are hidden — and so is the register. The issuer can no longer prove who holds what, enforce a concentration cap, or find a holder whose credential was revoked.

Saksi

Confidential to the market, answerable to the issuer and the regulator. Positions are shielded on-chain. Eligibility is proven inside the circuit. Compliance facts are proven on-chain without revealing the underlying positions.

03
How it works

Private in the middle, accountable at the edges.

Enter

Holder deposits CVA. A zero-knowledge proof shows their key is in the CVI registry and absent from the sanctions set. CCP pre-checks; Travel Rule data attaches.

Hold & transfer

Positions live as commitments. Amount and holder are hidden on-chain. Transfers re-prove eligibility every time — never once at onboarding.

Exit

Withdrawal releases CVA to a verified wallet only, through the same rule check, with attribution to the beneficial owner.

Answerability, without disclosure

Four proof types, each verified by an on-chain contract: an exact figure, a threshold (“this holder is under the cap”), an aggregate (“total exposure to this jurisdiction is under the limit”), and a two-sided range. Each is bound to an audit request registered on-chain, so a proof cannot be recycled.

Revocation as a live event

A revoked CVI enters the deny set. The holder's next proof fails non-membership and the position freezes — enforced by the verifier, not by an off-chain watcher running hours behind.

04
Why Cleanverse is essential

Cleanverse is not a feature here.
It is what the cryptography binds to.

PrimitiveHow Saksi uses it
CVI The membership set proven inside the circuit — not an off-chain KYC lookup before a transaction, but a constraint the proof cannot satisfy without a valid credential. The source key is pinned to the authenticated sender, so nobody enters as someone else.
CVA The only asset the pool accepts and the only thing it releases. The shielded leg moves A-Tokens strictly between verified wallets.
CCP Pre-transaction rule check at both edges. If the check blocks, no proof is built and nothing moves.
Travel Rule Attribution rides every entry and exit, with look-through to the beneficial owner rather than to the pool contract.
Reporting An audit pack where each line is a claim verified on-chain: proof, verification hash, and the audit request it was bound to — not a spreadsheet asking to be trusted.

Remove Cleanverse and there is no revocable, wallet-bound, bank-verified identity to prove membership in. The circuit has nothing to bite on and Saksi is an anonymous mixer — the exact thing this hackathon rules out of scope.

05
Architecture

Seven circuits, one pool, two consoles.

Circuits

Circom / Groth16 / BN254. Compliance (membership + sanctions non-membership), shielded transfer, trustless tree update, and four selective-disclosure variants.

Exported to Solidity verifiers via snarkjs. BN254 has EVM precompiles, so verification is cheaper here than on the platform this code came from.

Pool contract

Custodies CVA. Maintains the commitment set, the nullifier set, and the accepted-root history. Calls the verifiers on every state change and routes each edge transaction through CCP.

Deployment target: Monad. We probed all ten documented chains directly — Cleanverse's on-chain Validator answers on six of them, including the sponsor chain.

Consoles

Issuer — register health, concentration and jurisdiction limits, revocation actions, audit-pack export.

Regulator — raise an audit request on-chain, receive the proof, verify it against the live contract.

Honest scope

The zero-knowledge core is prior work, built and verified on Stellar testnet across eight contracts. The hackathon build is the port to EVM, the CVI and CVA gating, the Solidity pool, and the two consoles. Stated up front so the claim is checkable.

06
The demonstration

Two moments, both on-chain, both live.

One — prove without revealing

An issuer proves, on-chain, that no single holder exceeds the concentration cap and that aggregate exposure to a restricted jurisdiction stays under its limit.

Not one position is disclosed. The regulator's console verifies each proof against the live contract and prints the verification transaction hash.

Two — revoke and watch it freeze

A holder's CVI is revoked. Their next transfer is attempted in front of the panel and the proof fails non-membership.

The position freezes in the same block — enforced by the verifier contract, with nothing off-chain in the loop.

Most compliance demos show a transaction succeeding. The interesting one is the transaction that cannot succeed.

Who feels the pain

Issuers of tokenized notes, private credit, and funds who have a real concentration or jurisdiction limit to report against — and whose allocators will not accept a public book.

Who pays

The issuer, not the holder. Saksi attaches to an existing CVA issuance rather than competing with it, so the buyer is already inside the Cleanverse network.

08
Business model

A module, not a destination.

The disclosure layer is asset-agnostic. Any CVA issuer can attach a confidential register to an existing token without changing their issuance flow — which makes this infrastructure to be licensed, not a marketplace to be filled.

LineBasis
Register subscriptionPer issued asset, per year. Covers the pool deployment, the verifier set, and the issuer console.
Settlement feeA thin basis-point charge on value entering the shielded register.
Audit packPer regulator-facing report generated, where each line carries an on-chain verification hash.

Position in the Cleanverse ecosystem

Infrastructure Partner. Saksi does not issue, custody, or ramp. It makes an existing CVA issuance confidential without making it opaque — a capability the current stack does not offer, and one the consortium's own members are the natural first buyers of.

Pre-revenue. Pricing above is the proposed model, not observed willingness to pay.
